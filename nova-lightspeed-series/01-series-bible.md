# 01 — SERIES BIBLE + LORE + PHYSICS RULES

## LORE

- **Year 2100:** humanity achieves biological immortality. People become **invincible** (immune
  to injury, radiation, vacuum, extreme temperature) and **stop aging**.
- Decades later, with nothing left to fear, explorers begin pushing outward. The **Aurora**, a
  one-person ship with a **light-speed drive**, is built for the ultimate trip.
- **Nova** volunteers to be the first to attempt seeing the **entire universe**. She vlogs every
  leg of the journey and beams it back to Earth — part science log, part diary, part love letter
  to a home she's leaving behind.
- **Why this fixes consistency:** Nova never ages and never gets hurt, so her face, suit, and
  voice stay **identical forever** — by design. No costume changes, no aging, no continuity errors.

### The "present day" of the series
Use a running on-screen counter: **"EARTH YEAR 2147 · MISSION DAY 1"** in Episode 1, advancing
each episode. (You can pick any start year ≥ ~2120; 2147 is the default.)

## THE SCIENCE RULES (non-negotiable)

1. **Every number is real.** Sizes, distances, temperatures, speeds, and light-travel times must
   match real astronomy. Use the **DATA SHEET** below; never invent figures.
2. **Show the numbers.** Put key facts on screen as clean **data cards** (e.g., "☉ SUN — Ø 1,391,000 km
   · 109× Earth"). This is the channel's signature and what makes it bingeable + trustworthy.
3. **Light-speed = honest travel time.** At light speed, travel time to a target = its real
   light-distance. So "Moon in 1.3 seconds, Sun in 8m20s" is literally correct — lean into it.
4. **One artistic liberty, stated once:** real physics forbids a mass moving *at* light speed, and
   near-light speed causes extreme time dilation. The series treats the light-speed drive as the
   single sci-fi premise; **everything else stays factual.** (Optional: later episodes can play
   with time dilation as a story beat — see roadmap.)
5. **Round sensibly & cite units.** Use km, °C, AU, light-seconds/minutes/hours/years. Round for
   readability (e.g., Sun Ø ≈ 1.39 million km) but never distort scale.

## NOVA — the host

- **Look:** late-20s appearance (frozen at her 2100 age), warm friendly face, expressive brown
  eyes, light freckles, shoulder-length dark hair usually tied back. **White-and-teal flight suit**
  with an "AURORA" mission patch; helmet with a **gold reflective visor**; a small glowing pendant
  and a tiny plush mascot clipped to her chest (signature + future merch).
- **Voice:** warm, curious, a little witty, occasionally wistful. **One fixed AI voice forever.**
- **Personality:** endlessly curious scientist-explorer; explains big numbers like a friend, not a
  textbook; awestruck by beauty, honest about loneliness. Optimistic core.
- **Speech tics / brand lines:**
  - Intro: *"Hey Earth — it's Nova. Mission Day [X]. Look where we are today."*
  - When stating a fact: *"Real talk, real numbers:"* then the data card.
  - Sign-off: *"Still the same me, still heading out. Nova, signing off."*

## EPISODE FORMAT (repeatable template)

1. **Cold-open hook** (0–8s): a stunning view + a teaser line.
2. **Intro + where-we-are** (catchphrase + on-screen day/location).
3. **2–4 "leg" segments:** travel to the next object → reveal its **real data card** → Nova's
   reaction + one human-relatable comparison.
4. **One wonder/emotion beat** (a view that makes her pause).
5. **Cliffhanger tease** for next episode + sign-off.

Length: **6–9 min long-form**, then cut **3–5 Shorts** from the same footage (one object each).

## 📊 DATA SHEET — REAL NUMBERS (use these exactly)

**Speed of light:** 299,792 km/s ≈ **300,000 km/s** (~1,080 million km/h).
**1 AU** (Earth–Sun distance) = **149.6 million km** = **8.3 light-minutes**.

### Light-travel time from EARTH (≈ travel time at light speed)
| To | Distance | Light time |
|----|----------|-----------|
| Moon | 384,400 km | **1.3 seconds** |
| Sun | 149.6 million km | **8 min 20 s** |
| Mars (closest) | ~54.6 million km | ~3 min |
| Jupiter (avg) | ~5.2 AU | ~43 min (from Sun) |
| Neptune (avg) | ~30 AU | ~4.2 hours (from Sun) |
| Heliopause (Voyager 1) | ~163 AU | ~22.5 light-hours |
| Proxima Centauri | 4.24 light-years | **4.24 years** |

### Sizes (equatorial diameter) & key facts
| Body | Diameter | vs Earth | Headline fact |
|------|----------|----------|---------------|
| **Sun** | ~1,391,000 km | 109× | Holds 99.86% of the Solar System's mass; ~1.3 million Earths fit inside |
| **Mercury** | 4,879 km | 0.38× | Day +430°C, night −180°C |
| **Venus** | 12,104 km | 0.95× | Surface 465°C, 92× Earth pressure, sulfuric-acid clouds |
| **Earth** | 12,742 km | 1× | Home |
| **Mars** | 6,779 km | 0.53× | Avg −60°C; tallest volcano, Olympus Mons (~22 km) |
| **Jupiter** | 139,820 km | 11× | ~1,300 Earths fit inside; Great Red Spot storm wider than Earth |
| **Saturn** | 116,460 km | 9.1× | Rings ~280,000 km wide but only ~10 m–1 km thick |

| **Neptune** | 49,244 km | 3.9× | Fastest winds in the Solar System, ~2,100 km/h |

### Scale anchors (great for on-screen "wow")
- **Asteroid belt:** mostly empty — neighbouring asteroids are often **hundreds of thousands of km
  apart**; flying through, you'd likely hit nothing.
- **Voyager 1:** launched 1977, reached interstellar space in 2012 (~35 years). At light speed,
  that same distance takes **under a day**.
- **Oort cloud:** the Solar System's icy shell may extend to **~1 light-year+** — so even at light
  speed, **truly leaving the Solar System takes ~1 year**.
- **Nearest star (Proxima Centauri):** 4.24 ly → **4.24 years** at light speed.
- **Milky Way:** ~100,000 ly across, 100–400 billion stars.
- **Andromeda galaxy:** 2.5 million ly away (the farthest thing visible to the naked eye).
- **Observable universe:** ~93 billion ly in diameter.
